### Slide 1 — Cover
**Copy:**

**Design direction:** Near-black #0A0A0B, big serif statement in off-white, "TRIVIA." struck through or in muted gray, "a plan" in gold #C9A15C. Minimal.

### Slide 2 — Snapshot vs trajectory
**Copy:**
A snapshot tells you where you are.
It says nothing about where you're going.

$100k and shrinking is a very different situation from $100k and growing — but your app shows you the same number for both.

**Design direction:** Two identical "$100,000" figures side by side; one with a declining line, one with a rising line. Muted red vs gold.

### Slide 3 — The $100k parable
**Copy:**
Two people, both worth $100k.

One saves $5k a month. The other spends $2k more than they earn.

Same number today. In five years, one is near $400k — the other is near zero.

**Design direction:** Split screen, two trajectory lines diverging from a single point. Label the rates, not the people.

### Slide 4 — Three inputs
**Copy:**
Three inputs turn trivia into a plan:

1. WHERE YOU ARE — your number today
2. YOUR RATE — what you add (or subtract) each month
3. YOUR HORIZON — the date you care about

**Design direction:** Numbered list, clean typography, small gold numerals.

### Slide 5 — The math
**Copy:**
The math is simple:

start + (monthly rate × months) = your line.

Add a growth assumption and the line bends upward. Suddenly you have a trajectory — and trajectories have arrival dates.

**Design direction:** A simple line chart drawing itself left to right; the line crossing a dashed goal line labeled with a date.

### Slide 6 — Worked baseline
**Copy:**
Quick example: $100k today, adding $5k/month, over 10 years = $700k before any growth at all.

That's your baseline. Everything your money does beyond that is the plan working.

*(Illustrative example, not advice.)*

**Design direction:** Big number "$700,000" as hero, small print disclaimer.

### Slide 7 — Re-plot monthly
**Copy:**
Trajectories drift. Income changes, life happens.

So re-plot monthly. A plan isn't a prediction — it's a compass you keep checking against reality.

**Design direction:** One line with small correction markers along it, like a flight path adjusting course.
